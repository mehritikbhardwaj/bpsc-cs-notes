Java Basics

Introduction

  • Developed by James Gosling (father of Java), Mike Sheridan, Patrick Naughton at Sun Microsystems; released 1995. Earlier names: Green Talk, Oak. Now owned by Oracle (acquired Sun in 2010).
  • Motto: WORA — "Write Once, Run Anywhere". Source (.java) → compiler (javac) → bytecode (.class) → JVM executes it on any platform.
  • JDK (Java Development Kit = JRE + development tools) ⊃ JRE (Java Runtime Environment = JVM + libraries) ⊃ JVM (Java Virtual Machine). JIT compiler speeds up execution.
  • Features: simple, object-oriented, platform-independent, secure (no explicit pointers), robust (garbage collection, exception handling), multithreaded, portable, distributed.
  • Editions: Java SE (standard), Java EE / Jakarta EE (enterprise), Java ME (micro/mobile). Android apps traditionally use Java/Kotlin.

OOP in Java

  • Inheritance via extends; Java supports single, multilevel, hierarchical inheritance with classes; multiple inheritance only through interfaces (implements).
  • Abstract class — can have abstract and concrete methods; can't be instantiated. Interface — fully abstract contract (default/static methods allowed since Java 8).
  • Method overloading (compile-time) and overriding (runtime polymorphism).
  • Constructors: default and parameterised; no destructor (garbage collector frees memory).
  • Exception handling: try, catch, finally, throw, throws. Checked (IOException) vs unchecked (ArithmeticException, NullPointerException).
  • Packages: java.lang (default), java.util (Scanner, ArrayList, HashMap), java.io, java.sql (JDBC).
  • Strings are immutable; use StringBuilder/StringBuffer for mutable strings.
  • Threads: extend Thread or implement Runnable.

Society, Law & Ethics

Digital Footprint & Netiquette

  • Digital footprint — trail of data left online. Active (posts, forms you submit) and passive (data collected without your knowledge — cookies, IP, location).
  • Netiquette — good manners online: be respectful, don't spam, respect privacy and copyright, avoid cyberbullying, use strong passwords.
  • Digital citizenship — responsible use of technology.

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

  • Copyright — protects creative works (books, music, software code) — © symbol. In India: Copyright Act 1957.
  • Patent — protects inventions (generally 20 years). Patents Act 1970.
  • Trademark — protects brand names, logos (™, ®). Trade Marks Act 1999.
  • Plagiarism — using others' work without credit. Licensing: GPL (copyleft), Apache, MIT, Creative Commons (CC BY, CC BY-SA…).
  • FOSS: Free and Open Source Software — e.g. Linux, Firefox, LibreOffice, GIMP, Python, MySQL. Free Software Foundation by Richard Stallman (1985).

Cyber Crime & IT Act 2000

  • Information Technology Act, 2000 — India's main cyber law; passed 9 June 2000, effective 17 October 2000; amended in 2008. Gives legal recognition to e-records and digital signatures; based on UNCITRAL Model Law on e-commerce.
  • Key sections: 43 (damage to computer — compensation), 65 (tampering with source code), 66 (computer-related offences / hacking), 66C (identity theft), 66D (cheating by impersonation), 66E (privacy violation), 66F (cyber terrorism — up to life imprisonment), 67 (publishing obscene material). Section 66A was struck down by the Supreme Court (Shreya Singhal case, 2015).
  • Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) issues licences for digital signatures.
  • Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 — India's data protection law; Data Protection Board; rules notified in 2025.
  • Cyber crimes: hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyberbullying, cyberstalking, online fraud, child pornography, software piracy, ransomware.
  • Reporting: cybercrime.gov.in, helpline 1930.

E-waste & Health

  • E-waste — discarded electronic devices containing lead, mercury, cadmium. Management: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle. India: E-Waste (Management) Rules 2016, updated 2022.
  • Health concerns of technology: eye strain (20-20-20 rule), repetitive strain injury, bad posture, internet/gaming addiction, sleep problems. Ergonomics — designing workplaces for comfort.

Digital India & E-Governance

  • Digital India launched 1 July 2015. Initiatives: DigiLocker, UMANG app, Aadhaar, BHIM-UPI (NPCI, 2016), e-Hospital, e-Sign, BharatNet, CSC (Common Service Centres), MyGov, DIKSHA, SWAYAM.
  • NIC (National Informatics Centre, 1976) — government IT services. C-DAC (1988, Pune) — supercomputers. MeitY — Ministry of Electronics and IT.
  • Bihar: e-Kalyan, RTPS portal, Bihar Bhumi, Saat Nischay (7 resolves) — includes computer education in schools.
  • Gender & disability issues in computing: accessibility (screen readers, Braille keyboards), bridging the digital divide.

Emerging Trends

Big Data & Data Science

  • Extremely large data sets. 5 V's: Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, Value.
  • Tools: Hadoop, Apache Spark, NoSQL databases. Data mining, data warehousing (OLAP vs OLTP), data analytics.

Blockchain

  • Decentralised, distributed digital ledger; blocks linked by cryptographic hashes; immutable. Concept from Satoshi Nakamoto's Bitcoin paper (2008); Bitcoin launched 2009.
  • Uses: cryptocurrency, smart contracts (Ethereum, 2015), supply chain, land records, voting. India's Digital Rupee (e₹) — CBDC by RBI (pilot 2022).

AR, VR, MR

  • VR (Virtual Reality) — fully immersive computer-generated environment (Meta Quest headsets).
  • AR (Augmented Reality) — digital content over the real world (Pokémon Go, Snapchat filters).
  • MR (Mixed Reality) — real and virtual objects interact (Microsoft HoloLens, Apple Vision Pro).

Other Trends

  • Generative AI & LLMs — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude; generate text, images (DALL·E, Midjourney), code.
  • Quantum computing — uses qubits (superposition, entanglement). National Quantum Mission (India, 2023).
  • Edge / fog computing — processing near the data source to reduce latency.
  • 5G / 6G — 5G launched in India on 1 October 2022.
  • Robotics & drones, 3D printing (additive manufacturing), digital twins, Web 3.0 (decentralised web), Metaverse.
  • Grid computing — many computers work together on one task.
  • Smart cities, wearables, NLP (machine translation — Bhashini mission in India).
